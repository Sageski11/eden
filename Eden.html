The Chronicle

The Spirit of the Valley

Your people think and build for themselves. You cannot place their houses — you shape their world, answer their prayers and protect them. Their happiness becomes your Faith, and Faith pays for every miracle.

Shape the landRaise, lower, flatten and carve. Folk need flat, dry ground for homes and fields. Costs Faith by how much earth you move.
WaterPour water or open springs. Homes near water are happier, lake houses fish, mills turn — but floods destroy low houses.
BannersFree. “Settle here”, “Farm here”, “Worship here” steer where they build. “Keep clear” protects land. Each has a limit — click an existing banner with the same tool to remove it. Folk lay out streets from the square in front of their hall and build along them; a “Settle here” banner far from town makes them run a new lane out to it.
The land decidesLakes and rivers make fishers, docks and fish markets; forests feed hunters and sawmills; dry plains mean farms and hunting lodges. Wildlife breeds near forests — overhunt it and it vanishes.
CraftsFolk improve with practice. Woodcutting brings axes, then bows; fishing brings nets, then boats and sails; stone and woodwork unlock finer, more intricate buildings (J).
SkyRain, sunshine, snow, rainbows, thunderstorms — and meteors that carve craters and leave stone.
PrayersShown top right. Answer them for Faith and joy; ignored prayers make folk sad.
SeasonsFields grow spring to autumn; winter eats the stores. Summers can bring drought, springs bring floods.
DangersFires spread between thatched roofs (Rain or Bless saves them). From the Village era raiders come — Smite them, or cut them off with cliffs and water. Towers and castles shoot arrows.
The AgesStone Age → Bronze Age → Iron Age → Medieval → High Medieval → Industrial → Modern → Futuristic. Folk learn crafts by practice (Stonecutting, Woodworking… then Engineering and Science), and each age brings new buildings, miracles and ways to travel: huts become cottages, then townhouses, brick tenements, concrete towers and spires of light. Streets are paved, and railways and highways are laid across the land.
Piety & doubtHunger, fear, sickness, unanswered prayers and even pride make the folk doubt you. Watch Hearts of the folk to see why — and ease what troubles them before it breeds a false prophet.
False prophetsA prophet rises from doubt, raises a dark shrine and gathers followers whose deeds grow crueller. Answer with a Divine sign, Anoint a seer, or Silence the prophet (a strong cult will make them a martyr). Faith drains away while a cult thrives.
PetitionsThe folk reshape the land themselves — levelling plots, digging quarries — and ask your leave for the big works: draining a marsh, felling a forest, cutting a mountain, laying a railway or highway. Allow or Forbid. You may still raise, lower and carve the land yourself.
Other peoplesIn the Bronze Age a second people come into the valley and settle somewhere different — by the water, the forest or open land — so they live differently: fishers, hunters, farmers. You are the Spirit of both: switch with the tabs in the top bar or Tab. Faith is shared; each people have their own doubts, prophets and petitions. When one has what the other lacks, merchants walk between them and roads wear in. Judge one people and the other goes on.
JudgmentWhen a people are beyond saving, pass judgment: Deluge, Firestorm, Pestilence or Starfall. The faithful may flee. The reckoning is written in the Ledger of Ages, and a new age begins with a little of the old memory.
